Tangail, Bangladesh · Age 68

Amina
Khatun

Warp dresser · Cotton handloom

Amina begins before the loom begins. She counts fine cotton ends, finds a crossing thread by touch, and keeps hundreds of parallel lines under even tension. In this imagined workshop, three generations ask her to inspect a warp before it is mounted.

“The pattern can only travel straight when the foundation remembers its order.”
Signature skill
Warping and thread alignment
What she reads
Tension, spacing and broken ends
Tradition
Tangail cotton weaving

Rupganj, near Dhaka · Age 52

Kamal
Uddin

Ustad · Jamdani motif weaver

Kamal works in dialogue with an apprentice. The ground weft passes across the cloth; a separate pattern weft enters only where a flower or geometric form is needed. He holds the motif in memory, checking every small insertion against the rhythm of the loom.

“A motif floats because the hand knows exactly where to stop.”
Signature skill
Discontinuous extra-weft
Teaching pair
Ustad and shagred
Tradition
Dhakai Jamdani

Bishnupur, West Bengal · Age 38

Mitali
Das

Pattern weaver · Narrative silk

Mitali reads a pallu as a sequence rather than a surface. She watches the harness lift selected warp threads, checks the order of the pattern cards, and corrects a missed figure before it becomes part of the story. The loom assists repetition; her judgment protects the image.

“A card carries instructions. The weaver gives those instructions life.”
Signature skill
Jacquard card reading
What she protects
Motif sequence and selvedge
Tradition
Baluchari silk

Sambalpur, Odisha · Age 25

Rabi
Majhi

Bandha yarn preparer · Ikat registration

Rabi’s image exists in the yarn before it exists in cloth. He binds selected sections so they resist dye, opens and reties them for another colour, then aligns the patterned threads on the loom. A few millimetres of drift can soften a line—or become the unmistakable pulse of ikat.

“We do not print the pattern on cloth. We plan it inside the thread.”
Signature skill
Resist binding and registration
What he aligns
Pre-dyed warp patterns
Tradition
Sambalpuri Bandha

Anatomy of the craft

The vocabulary
inside a saree.

Handloom is a system of coordinated decisions. These terms help reveal where human skill enters the cloth—and where a tool supports rather than replaces it.

01

Warp

The lengthwise threads held under tension. Their count, order, spacing and strength establish the cloth’s foundation.

02

Weft

The crosswise thread carried through the shed. Beat by beat, it turns a field of parallel threads into fabric.

03

Warping

Measuring and arranging many yarn ends in sequence before they are dressed onto the loom. One error can travel through an entire saree.

04

Extra-weft

An additional thread used for motifs beyond the ground weave. In Jamdani it enters discontinuously, only where the design requires it.

05

Dobby & Jacquard

Human-operated loom attachments that control selected warp lifts for repeated borders or complex figures. They organize instructions; the weaver still controls the making.

06

Handloom vs powerloom

A handloom is operated through human motion and judgment. A powerloom is motor-driven, producing repeated cloth at industrial speed. A machine-like attachment alone does not make a handloom a powerloom.
